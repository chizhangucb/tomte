# Four labels that end in a human, and a dispatcher that reads only this repo's words

Four labels stop the factory and end with a person: `ready-for-human`, `hold`, `needs-human` and `agent:blocked`. They stay four. Two axes separate them and both are load-bearing. Who wrote it: `ready-for-human` and `hold` are a human's, written before or instead of the factory, while `needs-human` and `agent:blocked` are the factory's, recording what happened to a run. Merge an intent with an outcome and nobody can tell from a ticket whether the factory ever touched it. Then what else changed when the label landed, which is where the factory's two come apart:

- **`ready-for-human`**, a human's, one of the five triage roles in `docs/agents/triage-labels.md`. Tickets only, and nothing else changes: the factory never writes it. Amended 2026-09-11 (#210): nor reads it, since it left the labels that hold a ticket back. Cleared by doing the work, or by swapping labels to route the ticket to the factory after all.
- **`hold`**, a human's instruction that the factory start no work on a ready ticket (#169). Nothing else changes: `ready-for-agent` stays on and the pair reads as one. Removing it is the whole release. `docs/agents/triage-labels.md` is what a triager reads. Corrected 2026-09-11 (#185): the dispatcher, the retry handler and the reconciler all read it, on the ticket and on its open PR, and none starts an agent on a held subject. Standing down adds no `needs-human`, takes no `agent:*` off and spends no retry: it comments, and keeps a PR in the `agent:in-progress` it was in, which is what keeps a hold a person's and not an escalation. `PARKED_LABELS` is still exactly the pair below, the reconciler reading `hold` beside it rather than inside it. For work still carrying a state label the release lands at the reconciler's stuck deadline rather than on the `unlabeled` wake. Amended 2026-09-11 (#210): it stops no merge, and not two hand-offs on an open PR, update-branch's conflict hand-off and an agent run labelling its own PR `agent:review`; see Amendments.
- **`needs-human`**, the factory's, meaning it is done with this subject. A transition, not a note: `escalationLabels` takes every `agent:*` label off and `ready-for-agent` with them, so an escalated ticket says one thing rather than two. Reaching an open PR from the retry handler it also closes the PR or, on one the factory did not author, leaves it open with auto-merge disarmed (`prEscalation`, #174); the reconciler's own escalation relabels and comments and never closes. The retry cap is the usual route and not the only one, since the reconciler escalates a stranding it has missed twice and the audit opens a `needs-human` issue of its own on a miss. Cleared by fixing the ticket and then putting it back the way the escalation comment says, which is `needs-human` off, `factory:retry-1` off where a retry was spent, and `ready-for-agent` on again.
- **`agent:blocked`**, the factory's, meaning a human must look; ADR 0003 is where that one meaning was fixed, by its amendments of 2026-09-10 and by its conflict bullet's correction of 2026-09-11. Tickets and PRs. A note rather than a transition: it takes nothing off itself, leaves `ready-for-agent` and any `factory:retry-N` where they are, and spends no retry. That is not the same as the subject keeping its state, and reading it that way is the mistake to avoid: each agent workflow drops `agent:in-progress` on its way out and the sub-issue refusal takes `agent:implement` off before it labels, so a blocked subject usually carries `agent:blocked` alone. Who clears it is the one thing about this label that is not uniform, and the comment beside it is what says which case a reader is in. A failed step is cleared by re-adding that step's label, `agent:implement` or `agent:review`, which that run's own first step then takes `agent:blocked` off for. A conflict on a PR the factory did not author is its author's, cleared by resolving the conflict and taking the label off (#180). The sub-issue refusal names no label at all, because relabelling is not the fix and splitting the issue into tickets is.

Both of the factory's two are read as well as written, which is most of why collapsing them would cost more than it saves. `PARKED_LABELS` is exactly the pair, and a parked subject is one the reconciler leaves alone instead of repairing; `agent:blocked` is in `HANDED_OFF_LABELS` too, which is what makes update-branch skip a conflicting PR somebody is already holding rather than hand it to the implementer again. Collapsing them would mean recovering "does the factory still hold this" from the `agent:*` labels and "are retries left" from `factory:retry-N`, which is possible and is not worth the churn.

The second decision is about the vocabulary as a whole. The dispatcher decides on exact label strings, and every string it reads is one `scripts/onboard.sh` creates: `ready-for-agent`, `hold`, the four `agent:*` states and `needs-human`. None of GitHub's nine default labels is among them, which was an accident and is a decision now, with `factory/lib/labels.test.ts` failing if one ever enters those lists. A default carries a public meaning this repo does not control. `help wanted` means an outside contributor is welcome here, which is recruiting rather than routing, and a ticket can be `ready-for-human` and emphatically not want outside help. Wire one into `HOLD_LABELS` and everybody using it in its ordinary sense silently stops the factory, which is #169 with a wider blast radius: GitHub puts all nine on a repo before onboarding writes a single label of the factory's own, so a whole backlog arms at once with nobody having relabelled anything. The near miss is `wontfix`, both a GitHub default and one of the five triage roles, which the dispatcher happens not to read.

## Considered options

- **One label for "a human has it".** Rejected on both axes above: it loses whether the factory ever ran, and merges a transition that strips a ticket's state with a note that strips nothing. The tracker is what a maintainer reads, and which of the two is true is the question they need answered at a glance.
- **Prefixing every factory label so a collision with GitHub is impossible.** Rejected: `ready-for-agent`, `ready-for-human`, `needs-triage` and `needs-human` are the triage vocabulary a target's humans already use, and prefixing them forks it, while `agent:` is reserved for factory state and is the wrong thing to say about a human's instruction, which is why `hold` is unprefixed. So the rule stays a decision with a test behind it rather than a naming scheme.

## Consequences

- A fifth label that stops the factory and ends with a person amends this ADR, or it is the one nobody can place.
- The guard is a test over lists somebody else owns, so it has two holes worth naming: a tenth default GitHub ships is uncovered until somebody adds it to the nine, and a label list added somewhere the test does not enumerate is not checked at all.
- The rule is about GitHub's defaults, not about collisions in general. `hold` is an ordinary English word and unprefixed, so a target may already use it for something of its own; onboarding rewrites labels with `--force`, and `docs/factory/onboarding.md` is where that hazard is named.
- This repo carries no caller, so no dispatcher reads its own labels and all four are notes here rather than orders. The day it becomes a target is the day they start meaning what this ADR says they mean.
- Who writes `agent:blocked` is a list that grows, and this ADR fixes the meaning rather than the roster. #180 added update-branch declining a conflicting PR the factory did not author, and it is the clearest case of why the two sets matter: the label is in `HANDED_OFF_LABELS`, so the next run skips the PR instead of commenting again on every push to main, and in `PARKED_LABELS`, so the reconciler does not re-arm a PR it has already judged. Being in both is what makes a decline stick, and it is why adding a label here is never only adding a label.

## Amendments

### 2026-09-11: `hold` stops the factory starting work, never a merge (#210)

To stop a started ticket, close its PR and keep `hold` on; to resume, reopen the PR and take `hold` off. The reconciler's skip of a held PR's re-arm and update-branch went: a half-measure, since update-branch on a push to `main` already landed a held PR. Making `hold` stop merges was rejected: it needs a disarm, update-branch reading the ticket, a re-arm on release, and still leaves up to one sweep for a merge to land. Closing the PR has none of that. `ready-for-human` and `needs-triage` left `HOLD_LABELS` too: they were a backstop for old `needs-triage` + `ready-for-agent` pairs, and none is left.
